Metasurfaces · RF → THz → Optics

Unlocking the potential of electromagnetic phenomena, from RF to optics.

We manipulate electromagnetic waves at will using metasurfaces — ultrathin artificial electromagnetic surfaces that sculpt fields with sub-wavelength precision — for next-generation 6G communications, satellite links, terahertz systems, and high-resolution imaging.

Research

Wave control as a design problem

Our work spans microwave, millimeter-wave, terahertz, and optical regimes — treating mutual coupling and nonlocality not as nuisances, but as engineering resources.

Multi-band · RIS

Nonlocal multi-band reconfigurable intelligent surfaces

Frequency-selective structures interleaved on a single thin surface manipulate signals across multiple bands simultaneously — deliberately exploiting mutual coupling among unit cells as a design element for full half-space beam shaping.

Microwave · 6G

Cavity-excited nonlocal metasurface antennas

Resonant fields confined within a cavity excite a metasurface aperture directly, eliminating extensive feed networks while maintaining directive radiation — a compact, power-efficient platform for 6G front ends.

mm-wave · Radar

Spatially-dispersive metasurfaces and metagratings

Sparsely distributed metallic scatterers with wavelength-scale spacing yield angle-selective wave manipulation, targeting automotive radar and 6G links where the angular response is the specification.

THz

Nonlocal metasurface-aided THz leaky-wave antennas

Metasurfaces that remain robust over a broad range of incidence angles enable efficient, stable beam steering at terahertz frequencies — including simultaneous generation and manipulation of THz waves via nonlinear optics.

Computational

Deep-learning-assisted inverse design

Physics-aware inverse-design frameworks that explicitly account for mutual coupling accelerate metasurface synthesis, closing the loop between full-wave physics and AI-driven optimization.
